## What does a home repair contractor in {city} do?

A home repair contractor fixes damage and wear around the house: dry rot, water damage, sticking doors, failing windows, worn decks and fences, and items flagged on home inspection reports. In {city}, a licensed contractor can also pull permits for repairs that require them and fix the underlying cause so the problem doesn't return.

## What repairs do we handle?

### Dry rot and termite damage

Wood that stays damp eventually decays. Common spots include fascia, window and door trim, deck framing, and the bottom of exterior walls. We remove the damaged wood, find out why it was wet, and replace it with properly sealed material.

### Water damage

Leaks from plumbing, roofs, windows, or showers can damage drywall, subfloors, and framing. After the leak is fixed, we remove wet or damaged materials and rebuild, from framing through finished surfaces.

### Doors and windows

Doors that stick, don't latch, or let in drafts are usually fixable. When frames are rotted or units have failed, we replace them and repair the surrounding trim and wall finish.

### Decks, stairs, and fences

We repair or replace deck boards, joists, stair treads, railings, and fence sections, paying close attention to safety items like railings and stair connections.

### Inspection and pre-sale repairs

Buying or selling? We work from the inspection report and give you an itemized estimate so you can decide what to address and negotiate with clear numbers.

## Why fix the cause and not just the damage?

A repair that doesn't address why the damage happened will fail again. Before we replace rotted trim, for example, we look at:

- Whether water is getting behind the trim from above
- Whether sprinklers are hitting the wall
- Whether the original material was primed and sealed on all sides
- Whether flashing or caulking has failed

Fixing those issues is what makes a repair last.

## When should a repair be handled right away?

Some problems get worse and more expensive quickly. Call promptly if you notice:

1. **Active leaks** or water stains that are growing
2. **Soft or spongy floors**, especially near bathrooms and kitchens
3. **Loose railings or stairs**, which are a safety hazard
4. **Doors or windows that suddenly stick**, which can signal moisture or settling

## Is a handyman or a licensed contractor the right choice?

California limits the size of jobs that can be done without a contractor's license. For anything beyond small tasks, and especially work involving structure, water damage, or permits, a licensed contractor offers accountability, insurance, and code compliance. You can verify any license on the CSLB website.

## How can I prevent common repairs?

Many of the repairs we see start with water, so a little seasonal maintenance goes a long way:

- **Keep gutters and downspouts clear** and make sure water drains away from the foundation.
- **Adjust sprinklers** so they don't spray siding, stucco, fences, or wood trim.
- **Re-caulk and repaint exterior trim** before the finish wears through to bare wood.
- **Check under sinks and around toilets** for slow drips a few times a year.
- **Look at decks and railings** each spring for loose fasteners, soft boards, or wobbling posts.

Catching small issues during routine checks keeps them from turning into larger repairs. If you'd like, we can walk the property and give you a prioritized list of what needs attention now and what can wait.

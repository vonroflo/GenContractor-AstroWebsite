## What does a stucco contractor in {city} do?

A stucco contractor installs, repairs, and refinishes exterior stucco walls. In {city}, that most often means fixing cracks and damaged areas, patching around new windows and doors, correcting water problems behind the stucco, and applying new color coats or a full re-stucco so the exterior is sealed and looks uniform.

## What stucco services do we offer?

### Crack and damage repair

Stucco is rigid, so it cracks as a house settles and moves with temperature changes. Small cracks can be routed, filled, and refinished. Larger damaged areas are cut back to sound material, the paper and lath are repaired, and new stucco is applied in coats to match the wall.

### Patching after window and door replacement

Retrofit and new-construction window replacements often leave a band of damaged stucco. We repair the opening with proper flashing and paper laps first, then patch the stucco and blend the texture so the new window looks like it was always there.

### Water damage behind stucco

Stucco itself isn't waterproof; the weather-resistive barrier behind it is what keeps water out. When flashing fails or the barrier is torn, water can reach the framing. We open the wall, repair damaged sheathing or framing, rebuild the barrier and flashing, and then restore the stucco.

### Color coats and re-stucco

If your exterior has many patches, faded color, or a texture you no longer like, a new finish coat can make the whole house look new. For heavily damaged or very old systems, a full tear-off and new three-coat stucco may be the better option.

## How do you match existing stucco?

Matching is part craft and part planning. Texture can usually be matched by hand on site. Color is harder, because older stucco has weathered unevenly. To avoid a visible patch, we usually:

- Match the texture of the surrounding wall
- Extend the finish to a natural break such as a corner or trim line
- Apply a color coat or paint to the full wall plane

We'll talk through what's realistic for your wall before work starts.

## What causes stucco problems?

Most stucco issues trace back to a few causes:

1. **Movement**: normal settling and temperature swings
2. **Water**: failed flashing, missing weep screed, or poor drainage at the base of walls
3. **Impact**: ladders, landscaping equipment, or vehicles
4. **Installation**: too few coats, poor curing, or missing control joints

Fixing the cause, not just the crack, is what keeps a repair from coming back.

Costs depend on the size of the damaged area, whether there's hidden water damage, how much texture and color matching is needed, and access, such as second-story walls that need scaffolding. Small cosmetic repairs cost far less than repairs that involve opening the wall. We'll give you a written estimate with the scope spelled out, and if we find hidden damage once the wall is open, we'll show you and discuss it before going further.

## When should I call a professional?

Call for an inspection if you see cracks wider than a credit card, cracks with staining, bulging or hollow-sounding areas, or interior signs of moisture on exterior walls. Catching water intrusion early is far less expensive than repairing rotted framing later.

## How do I maintain stucco?

Stucco is low-maintenance, but a little attention keeps it performing:

- **Keep sprinklers off the walls**; constant wetting at the base of stucco leads to staining and damage.
- **Keep soil and mulch below the weep screed** at the bottom of the wall so trapped water can drain out.
- **Seal small cracks** before water can work into them.
- **Repaint or recoat** when the finish starts to fade or chalk.

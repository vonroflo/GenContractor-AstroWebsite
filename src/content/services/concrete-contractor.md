A concrete contractor plans, forms, pours, and finishes concrete for driveways, patios, walkways, foundations, and retaining walls. ## What concrete work do we handle?

We take on residential concrete projects of most sizes, from a single set of steps to a full driveway replacement or a foundation for a new addition.

### Driveways

A driveway carries the weight of vehicles every day, so the base matters as much as the surface. We remove the old slab, regrade for drainage away from the house, compact the subgrade, and reinforce the new pour. Control joints are planned so that the cracking all concrete does happens in straight, clean lines.

### Patios and outdoor living areas

A new patio extends your living space outdoors. We help you size it for furniture and traffic, slope it gently away from the home, and pick a finish that suits how you'll use it. Broom finish is practical and slip-resistant; stamped or colored concrete gives a more decorative look.

### Walkways, steps, and landings

Uneven walkways and crumbling steps are trip hazards. We rebuild them with consistent rise and run, proper landings at doors, and a textured finish for traction.

### Foundations and slabs

Foundations for room additions, ADUs, and garages are structural work. We build them to the engineered plans, coordinate inspections, and make sure the slab is level and square before framing starts.

## Why does base preparation matter so much?

Most concrete failures start underneath the slab. Soil that isn't compacted settles over time, and the concrete above it cracks or sinks. Water that collects under a slab can wash out the base or cause heaving.

Before any pour, we:

1. Excavate to the right depth for the slab and base material
2. Compact the subgrade and base in lifts
3. Set forms to the correct slope for drainage
4. Place reinforcement on supports so it ends up inside the slab, not at the bottom

That work is invisible once the concrete is down, but it's the difference between a slab that lasts and one that needs replacing early.

## How should I care for new concrete?

New concrete gains strength over several weeks. For the first week, keep vehicles off, avoid dragging heavy objects across it, and follow any curing instructions we leave. After that, routine care is simple: sweep and rinse it, clean up oil spills promptly, and consider a penetrating sealer every few years for decorative finishes.

## Why choose a licensed contractor for concrete?

In California, a contractor doing work over the state's minor-work threshold must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board. A licensed contractor carries the required bonding, follows building codes, and can pull permits. You can check any contractor's license on the CSLB website before you hire, and we encourage you to check ours.

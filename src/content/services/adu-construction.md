## What does an ADU contractor in {city} do?

An ADU contractor builds accessory dwelling units: second homes on a residential lot, either detached, attached to the main house, or converted from existing space such as a garage. In {city}, that includes evaluating your lot, coordinating plans and permits, managing utility connections, and building the unit through final inspection.

## What types of ADUs can you build?

### Detached ADU

A detached ADU is a standalone home, usually in the backyard. It offers the most privacy and flexibility in layout, and involves a new foundation, framing, roof, and utility runs from the main house or street.

### Attached ADU

An attached ADU shares a wall with the main house but has its own entrance, kitchen, and bathroom. It can be a good fit when yard space is limited or when tying into existing utilities is easier.

### Junior ADU

A junior ADU (JADU) is created within the existing footprint of the home, such as converting a bedroom suite. JADUs have size limits and specific rules, but they can be one of the most affordable ways to add a unit.

### Garage conversion

Converting an attached or detached garage reuses the existing foundation and structure. The work typically includes insulation, new windows and doors, a raised or leveled floor, utilities, and fire separation where needed.

## Why build an ADU?

Homeowners build ADUs for many reasons:

- **Rental income** to offset a mortgage
- **Housing for family**, such as aging parents or adult children
- **A home office or studio** separate from the main house
- **Added property value** and flexibility for the future

## What affects ADU cost in {city}?

The main factors are:

- **Type of unit**: conversions generally cost less than new detached units
- **Size and layout**
- **Site conditions**: slope, access for equipment, and soil
- **Utilities**: distance to connections and whether your electrical panel needs an upgrade
- **Finishes**: cabinets, flooring, fixtures, and appliances
- **Plans, engineering, and fees**

We'll give you a feasibility review and a detailed written estimate before you commit to plans.

## How does the ADU permit process work?

California has made ADUs easier to permit, but the process still involves several steps:

1. **Feasibility**: confirming what your lot allows
2. **Plans**: architectural drawings, structural engineering, and energy calculations
3. **Plan check**: city review, followed by corrections if needed
4. **Permit issuance**
5. **Inspections** at foundation, framing, utilities, insulation, and final

We coordinate each step and keep you updated on where things stand.

## Why work with one contractor for the whole ADU?

An ADU is a complete home: foundation, framing, roofing, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, and finishes. Having one licensed general contractor responsible for the whole job means a single schedule, a single point of contact, and clear accountability from start to finish.

## What should I decide before starting an ADU?

A few early decisions shape the design, cost, and schedule of an ADU, so it helps to think them through before plans are drawn:

- **Who will live there?** A rental unit, a family member, and a home office each call for different layouts and finishes.
- **How much privacy do you need?** This often decides between a detached unit, an attached unit, or a conversion.
- **Where will the entrance and parking be?** Access affects both the site plan and how the unit feels day to day.
- **What's your budget range?** Knowing it early lets us recommend the type and size of unit that fits.
- **How will you handle utilities?** Shared versus separate meters affects cost and future flexibility.

We'll talk through these during the feasibility visit so the plans reflect how you actually want to use the space.

## What does a waterproofing contractor in {city} do?

A waterproofing contractor keeps water out of the parts of a home where it causes damage: foundations, retaining walls, decks, balconies, and showers. In {city}, that means investigating leaks, correcting drainage, installing membranes and flashing, and repairing anything water has already damaged.

## Where does water usually get into a home?

Water follows gravity and the path of least resistance. The most common entry points we see are:

- **Foundation walls and slabs**, especially on hillside lots or where soil holds water
- **Retaining walls** without drainage behind them
- **Decks and balconies** over living space, where coatings have worn or flashing has failed
- **Windows and doors** with missing or reversed flashing
- **Showers** with failed pans or grout and no proper membrane

Often the stain you see isn't where the water is entering. That's why investigation comes first.

## What waterproofing services do we provide?

### Foundation and below-grade waterproofing

For basements, crawlspaces, and walls below grade, we focus on managing water outside the wall: excavating where needed, applying a membrane, adding drainage board and perforated drain pipe, and making sure grading carries water away from the house.

### Retaining walls

Water pressure behind a retaining wall can push it out of plumb or force moisture through it. We install drainage behind walls and waterproof the back face when the wall is next to living space.

### Decks and balconies

Decks over living space need a continuous waterproof membrane with proper slope and flashing at every edge and penetration. We repair or replace membranes and fix the flashing details where most leaks start.

### Showers and wet areas

A tiled shower is only as watertight as the membrane behind the tile. When shower pans fail, we remove the tile, replace damaged materials, install a new waterproof system, and flood test it before new tile goes on.

## How do you find the source of a leak?

We start with the history: when the leak appears, how heavy the rain was, and whether it happens with irrigation or showers. Then we inspect the likely paths, use moisture meters, and, where needed, run a controlled water test on one area at a time. Finding the true source avoids paying for repairs in the wrong place.

Costs vary more than in most trades because the work ranges from resealing a balcony to excavating along a foundation. The main factors are access, the area to be waterproofed, how much damage needs repair, and whether drainage has to be added. ## Can waterproofing prevent mold?

Mold needs ongoing moisture. Stopping the water source and drying out affected areas removes the conditions mold needs. If there is existing mold, we'll tell you and recommend testing or remediation by a specialist where appropriate.

## What can I do to reduce water problems?

Waterproofing works best alongside good drainage. A few things homeowners can do:

- **Keep gutters and downspouts clear**, and extend downspouts so they discharge well away from the foundation.
- **Check grading** around the house; soil should slope away from the walls, not toward them.
- **Adjust irrigation** so sprinklers don't soak foundation walls, retaining walls, or stucco.
- **Keep deck and balcony drains clear** of leaves and debris.
- **Watch for early signs** such as efflorescence, peeling paint at the base of walls, and musty smells, and call before they spread.

These habits won't replace a failed membrane, but they reduce the load on your waterproofing and help it last longer.
